1 · What

An assistant on top of the canvas, not instead of it

M2 is the MCP surface the planning meeting asked for: ask a question about the trip, and act on the answer.

The assistant is the thinnest of the four documents on purpose.

  • Its surface already exists. M1 ships a read-only Ask panel; M2 gives it verbs.
  • Its context is free. Destination plus date window is the session state, so "is anything open that week" needs no follow-up question.
  • Its data is the moat. We poll availability; a model does not.
  • What it must never become is the primary surface. Comparing six campgrounds is a spatial act, and a chat hands you one answer at a time.
2 · Market

The rails exist, and the category slot is open

Between drafts of this document the MCP bet stopped being speculative. What follows is from a market pass on 18 Aug 2026; sources are linked where a claim would otherwise be a guess.

Four facts that change how much M2 matters.

  • Every major client now speaks MCP. OpenAI adopted it in March 2025 and built the ChatGPT Apps SDK on it; the protocol moved to the Linux Foundation in December 2025 with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and AWS as founding members. One server reaches every assistant.
  • The consumer wave arrived in April 2026. Claude added a batch of lifestyle connectors — AllTrails, Booking.com, Tripadvisor, Resy, Uber. AllTrails-in-Claude (May 2026) is our template: conversational search over proprietary outdoors data, no account required to read.
  • Nobody owns camping. The field is Recreation.gov scrapers and hobby servers — none listed in the Claude connectors directory or the ChatGPT app directory. The first credible camping connector takes the category slot in both.
  • Assistants are already doing reservations. OpenTable, Resy and Yelp commit real bookings inside ChatGPT as of this month. The write-verb pattern we planned is now the norm, not a novelty.

Why this doesn't disintermediate us

  • Expedia reports assistant-referred traffic is small but converts better than other channels. The agent is a front door, not a replacement.
  • Reading availability needs no account. Creating a watch does. Every MCP conversation that ends in a watch ends in a Roadtrip account — the watch list is the retention loop, wherever the question was asked.
  • The pattern across industries is the same: Zillow's saved search, Hopper's fare watch, Keepa's price alert. The company that holds the standing watch holds the user.

Why handoff stays the booking model

  • OpenAI killed in-chat checkout in March 2026 after inventory-accuracy failures, and rebuilt around discovery in chat, transaction on the merchant. The market tested our exact question and answered it.
  • Recreation.gov has no agentic checkout and its ecosystem norm — Campflare, Campnab — is notify-only: the agent watches, the human books. That posture is also what keeps us clean when anti-bot and anti-resale law arrives, as it already has for restaurant reservations.
  • So the handoff verb is a deep link with dates and site prefilled — never a purchase.
3 · Dependency

Blocked on M1, not on the model

This is the whole of the M2 schedule risk, and none of it is model choice.

Set the window. Filter. Compare. Watch. Hand off. Those verbs get their names, arguments and guarantees by building M1 — not by choosing a model. M1 slipping moves M2 by the same amount, one for one.

The two that act are the two that need a confirmation model. Creating a watch is reversible and cheap; a cart hand-off is neither. That is a design problem, and it is the only substantial design work M2 carries.

What the questions people actually ask add to the list

Put the six personas in front of an assistant and their first questions decompose almost entirely into the verbs above — "open within three hours of Sacramento this weekend, fits a 32-foot trailer with hookups" is search + filter + distance. What's left over is four verbs the table doesn't have yet, each one anchored to a question we currently cannot answer.

And one deliberate refusal. Bare cancellation alerts are now a free commodity — Campflare charges nothing and scans every 15–45 seconds. The defensible thing is not the alert; it is the alert inside a plan: wired to backups, aware of the rig, honest about the season. Verbs that are pure content — packing lists, hike ideas, park lore — stay out; a general model does those fine without us, and answering beyond our data is the failure mode.

5 · Hyper search

The constraint query, answered by the assistant

This is the query the original strategy was built around, and it lands here rather than in M1. M1a lets you read one destination. Hyper search asks one question across all of them.

"Show me campsites within four hours, on new-moon weekends, that are currently bookable."

  • One sentence joining drive time, a date rule nobody controls, and live availability. No existing product answers it.
  • It is a poor fit for a form. Six filters and a calendar cannot express "new-moon weekends" without a bespoke control per constraint, and the next constraint needs another one.
  • It is a natural fit for language plus tools. The assistant parses the sentence; the verbs do the work; the canvas shows the result.
  • The answer is a map, not a paragraph. The assistant narrows the canvas and says what it did. Choosing still happens spatially.

Why it belongs in M2, not M1

What M1a already gives it

  • A destination index, so "within four hours" has somewhere to search.
  • Availability at three grains, so "bookable" is a fact rather than a guess.
  • Drive time from a reference point, so distance is measured rather than estimated.
  • Filters over normalised amenities, so physical fit is expressible.
  • A date window in the URL, so a result is shareable the moment it exists.

What it still needs

  • Date rules as data. New moons, permit release dates, foliage peaks, meteor showers. Each is a calendar we generate or ingest, not a filter someone types.
  • A cross-destination query path. M1a answers within one destination; this asks across many, which is an indexing and caching problem.
  • An honest empty. "Nothing matches all four" has to offer which constraint to relax, in the same breath.
  • A result the canvas can hold. Scattered pins across several states is a different map problem from one park.

The dependency worth noting. "Within four hours of home" needs an origin, and an origin is part of the trip object in M1b. Hyper search can ship against a destination or a map centre first, and gets better the moment a trip has a starting point.

Why this is the moat, stated plainly.

  • A general assistant is good at "what should I pack for a rainy weekend" and hopeless at "is Kirk Creek bookable next Saturday" — it will guess rather than check.
  • Plugged into our verbs over MCP, the same assistant answers the second question correctly, because we hold the data.
  • That combination is the thing a competitor cannot copy by writing content. It is why the strategy called this an integration problem.
  • Kill criteria, unchanged: if a competitor ships a constraint filter set and a permit calendar, this edge is gone.
6 · Surface

Where the assistant sits, and how much it does

Three years of travel products shipping assistants is a usable experiment log. The short version: our founding principle — the assistant narrows, the canvas chooses — is the one pattern that survived contact with users.

What survived, 2023–2026

  • AI embedded in the surface users already had. Kayak's chat sidebar became a smarter search box; Booking's wins were filters and Q&A, not the chatbot.
  • Answers that are artifacts. Tripadvisor's AI builds a saved itinerary, not a transcript — and those members generate 2–3× the revenue.
  • AI in the map itself. Google's Ask Maps ties every answer to a customized map; Mindtrip, the strongest AI-travel startup, is a chat–map split screen. Nobody credible ships chat alone.
  • Proactive watching, human buying. Hopper's watch-and-notify is beloved precisely because the human keeps the purchase.

What died, and how

  • The chatbot as primary planner. Expedia's first-generation assistant was discontinued; the stated reason was answers not grounded in live inventory. Users forgave nothing.
  • Autonomous booking. Only 2–8% of leisure travelers will let an AI book; about half will accept suggestions. The market Skift calls "agents for a consumer that doesn't exist."
  • Invented places. A couple drove 300 km to a cable car that never existed; a general model's 90% itinerary-error rate is our marketing wedge, not our product risk — unless we repeat it.

The rules this buys us

R1

Every answer ends on the canvas

The assistant's terminal state is a filtered, annotated map — never prose. A chat that answers terminally trains people off the surface where comparing, and everything downstream of comparing, happens.

R2

Watches are proposed, never assumed

The accepted framing everywhere is "Want me to watch this?" as a one-tap card the user arms. No product ships silently self-arming watches, and we will not be the first.

R3

Confirmation is tiered, not uniform

One tap and an undo for a watch; an explicit confirmation naming site, dates and destination for a hand-off. Confirming everything breeds the reflexive click-through that confirms nothing.

R4

The blank box is never blank

The best-documented chat failure is people not knowing what to ask. Seed the panel with prompts computed from the viewport: "which of these have July 4 openings," "sites here that fit 32 ft."

The one-line summary from every adjacent industry.

  • Real estate: the saved search is the retention engine, and Redfin markets alert latency as a headline claim. Our polling speed is legible marketing, not just infrastructure.
  • Flights: Hopper turned the anxiety between watching and booking into 40% of its revenue. We are not building fintech in M2 — but the watch-to-book window is where future monetization lives.
  • Price trackers: Keepa is trusted because its data age is visible. Freshness honesty is the product, not a caveat on it.
  • Everywhere: the standing watch is the account. Every assistant conversation, ours or an MCP client's, should try to end by creating or enriching one.
7 · Done

How we know M2 landed

1

It answers from our data

Every factual claim traces to a verb above. An answer the canvas cannot show is an answer we do not give.

2

It inherits context

Destination and date window come from the session. Nobody restates the week they are free.

3

Acting is deliberate

Nothing is created, changed or handed off without an explicit confirmation naming what will happen.

4

The map stays the product

The assistant narrows and explains. Choosing still happens on the canvas, where six options can be held at once.

5

It says when it knew

Every availability claim carries the timestamp of the poll behind it, in-product and over MCP alike. Stale answers presented as current are the one documented way products like this die.

6

It is findable where agents look

The server is live, OAuth'd, and submitted to the Claude connectors directory and the ChatGPT app directory. Unlisted is unlaunched.
