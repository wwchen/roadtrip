# The Place screen's rules

The interface answers one question, *where am I sleeping*, and it answers it with
two knobs: **where** and **when**. Everything on this page follows from that. The
components beside it in the catalog (`Sheet`, `PlaceVerdict`, `NightsStrip`,
`SiteMatrix`, `AlertRow`) are the rules in executable form; this page is the
reasoning you would otherwise have to recover from them.

Phase 1 builds the Place screen only. The other two screens appear below because
the Place screen is defined against them.

## Where and when

*Where* is **pinned** (one place) or **open** (an area). *When* has three values,
not two: **a night**, **a window** of nights, or **open**. The third value is not
symmetry. A trip pins a route and a five-night window and then answers night by
night, so a two-value *when* breaks on the first state the design has.

> The narrowest pinned knob titles the screen. The widest open knob becomes the
> answer.

Pinning a knob is a navigation and unpinning it is going back. There is no tab bar,
no menu and no modal routing. A notification that fires opens the Place screen with
both knobs pinned, which is the one state the user asked to be told about.

The Place screen has no map. The place is pinned, so geography is settled and
showing it again would be the duplication the design exists to avoid.

## Three heights, and a fact belongs to exactly one

`Sheet` has three detents. Each `SheetSection` declares the detent at which its
content first appears, `from` is required, and content with no declared height does
not compile. That is what turns "a fact belongs to exactly one height" from a
convention into a type error.

### Full replaces the strip with the grid

The same fact at more detail sits in the same place in the order. At Full the
nights strip and the sites-open list give way to `SiteMatrix`, the site-by-date
grid: every campsite against every night of the stay, with the Loop, Type and
free-text filters and the sort. The place's own detail then scrolls *below* it, so
nothing Full used to carry is lost. Half is unchanged, and so is Peek.

That is a swap, not an addition, because the strip and the grid answer the same
question — *which nights* — and "a fact belongs to exactly one height" applies to
the two renderings of one answer as much as it does to two different answers. A
sheet showing both would state the week twice.

The grid is the one place the sheet's own width cap moves: 420px at Peek and Half,
where it sits over the map, and the window's width at Full, where it covers the map
anyway. On a phone 420px is the whole screen, so the week scrolls sideways under a
frozen Site column. The grid caps its own height and scrolls its rows, so the place
detail stays a short scroll below it rather than two hundred rows below it.

The header compacts as the sheet rises, to the name and the stay answer's status
line. The name is said once at every height. The verdict is the answer, so it rides
at every height too; what compacts away is the facts that decide it (the drive time,
the price, the fits line, the reasons and the freshness line), which stay at Peek.

Half does not carry a price. The wire holds one `price` per POI, a range on the POI
detail, and nothing per night or per site. A place-level *from* price belongs to
Peek, said once.

### Arriving from the map opens at Half

`ARRIVAL_DETENT` maps `from-map` to Half and `cold` to Peek. Peek's content is the
card the user has just tapped on the map, so opening there would show them the same
deciding facts twice. Peek is for cold arrivals only: a notification, a deep link, or a
place reached by name rather than off the map.

Every type honours that, not only the campgrounds with a stay to check: a charger, a
gym, a trailhead and a state park reached from the map open at Half too. The one
exception is named rather than assumed — `PEEK_ONLY_CATEGORIES` in `PlaceSurface`
holds the dropped pin, whose every fact fits in Peek, so raising it would open a
sheet with nothing new in it.

### One component, two consumers

The Where card and the Place peek both render `PlaceVerdict`, so the two cannot
phrase a verdict differently. That buys consistency and nothing else. A shared
component rendered in two places on one path says the same thing twice, reliably.
Non-repetition is enforced by the detent a screen opens at and by `SheetSection`
levels, which is why the two rules are separate and neither stands in for the other.

## A stay claim never comes from day rollups

This is the rule that would otherwise ship a Reserve button that makes a false
promise.

There are two reads, and they answer different questions.

- **The stay check**, `POST /api/pois/availability/stays`, answers *how does this
  stay look here*. Its `open_sites` is the number of campsites bookable online
  **for every night of the stay**.
- **The day grid**, `GET /api/pois/{id}/campsites/availability`, answers *how does
  each night look*. Its `AvailabilityDay` rollups are per-night counts.

Three sites open on Saturday and three open on Sunday can mean that no single site
is open on both nights. Summing, or taking the minimum of, the two rollups produces
a number that reads like a stay and is not one. A Reserve button behind that number
sends someone to a booking page for a stay that cannot be made.

So the rule is structural:

- "Open both nights", the verdict's count, and anything that sits behind a Reserve
  button read the stay check. In the code that is `usePlaceStay` feeding
  `PlaceVerdict`.
- The nights strip and the site-by-date grid read the day grid and are per-night
  context only. In the code that is `usePlaceNights` feeding `NightsStrip` and
  `NightSites` at Half and `SiteMatrix` at Full. Neither is ever summed into a stay
  claim. The grid renders cells, which is per-campsite-per-night and is exactly
  what cells are for; what it may not do is add them up into a sentence about the
  whole stay.

The grid carries the same discipline inside itself. `nightOf` reads a night off its
cells; a day with no cells is `unknown`, and a day that claims to be open without an
open cell is `unknown` too. The rollup never stands in for a missing cell.
`SiteMatrix` applies it one level down: `cellState` is a straight lookup of
`day.cells[campsiteId]`, and a night the wire never answered is `unknown` rather
than the rollup of the nights it did answer.

### One read serves both detents

`usePlaceNights` makes **two** requests and no more — the screen as a whole makes
others, the stay check among them, but these two are the whole of the per-night
read: the week from `GET /api/pois/{id}/campsites/availability`, and the campsite
catalog from `GET /api/pois/{id}/campsites`. It publishes `days`, `dates`,
`campsites` and `siteNames` off them, and the strip at Half and the grid at Full
are both fed from that one view. Neither owns a query.

This is the invariant most likely to be broken by the next feature, so it is worth
stating plainly: **raising the sheet to Full must not issue a request.** A grid
that fetched its own week would be a second rendering of the same nights that can
disagree with the first, and a grid that fetched its own catalog would be the
duplicate query this one already replaced. The catalog is a POI's fixed inventory
and the strip already needed it for site names, so the metadata the Loop and Type
filters read was on the wire before the grid existed.

The grid's columns are `dates` — the stay's own nights, the same list the strip
iterates — so the two detents cannot disagree about which nights are in view. That
is also why the grid has no week paging: paging would either move the window the
strip reads, leaving the strip and the verdict describing different nights, or give
the grid a window of its own, which is the second read. Changing the window is the
date picker's job.

Every status the stay check can return has a sentence already, in `stayCopy` in
`lib/strings.ts`, and the sheet renders the branch the server gives it. The browser
invents no vocabulary of its own. A count dressed up as prose is not one of the
branches.

### A grid the booking site would not answer

The same rule binds the grid's failures. A refused read has a server-stated code,
`lib/availability-errors.ts` turns that code into the sentence written for it, and
`usePlaceNights` carries it out as `failure`. The sheet shows that sentence in place
of whichever reading of the week the detent would otherwise carry, the strip at Half
or the grid at Full — not a week of `No data` chips, and not an empty grid, which are
claims the server never made standing in for the reason it did state.

The retry follows the same source: it is offered for a failure on the provider's end
and withheld while the provider is rate-limiting us, because asking again is the one
thing that cannot help there.

## Getting out of the sheet

`Sheet` takes an optional `onClose`. Given one it draws a close control beside the
grabber — both 44px — and Escape inside the sheet dismisses it. The map surface
passes `clearSelectedPoi`; the routed `?poi=<id>` page passes nothing, because it has
nowhere to close to, and a gated control that cannot act is worse than no control.

The sheet is not modal. Focus is never trapped, the map keeps its gestures, and the
scrolling body is a tab stop so a keyboard can reach prose that holds no controls of
its own.

## Where Half ends and Full begins

`POI_BLOCK_GROUPS` in `domain/poi/blocks.ts` lists the blocks in their one fixed
order, and `PlaceSheet` splits that list into Half and Full after the `glance`
group (`HALF_GROUP_COUNT`).

That is **not** `RULE_BEFORE_GROUP`, although the constant is right there and
invites it. `RULE_BEFORE_GROUP` sits after `gettingThere` and `goodToKnow`, and
those two blocks answer *what is it like when I arrive*, which is Full. Splitting
at the rule would pull them up into Half, where the question is *which nights*.
The hairline keeps its own meaning in the block order, and the detent boundary
is a separate decision that happens to share a list.

The block order itself does not change. A type omits blocks; it never reorders them.
What the sheet changes is that the groups become detents instead of scroll position.

## What the sheet leaves out

**The sheet renders no `hero` and no `identity`** (`SHEET_OMITS`).

- `hero` is a 176px photograph. At the top of a 395px Half detent it pushes
  `actions`, which holds Reserve, below the fold, and Reserve is the one thing Half
  must guarantee. Moving it into Full would put it after `glance` on screen, which
  breaks the older and stronger rule that the sequence never changes. So it is
  omitted, and a type omitting a block is already how the order works.
- `identity` is the name above the type's eyebrow. The sheet's header already owns
  both, so rendering them again in the body is the repetition the detents exist to
  prevent.

## The header's one secondary line

The header carries the name and exactly one secondary line, and that line is the
type's own eyebrow, verbatim, from the same source the identity block reads:
`Campground · <agency>`, `Charger · Tesla`, `National park`. Who manages a place
tells a camper which booking system and which rules to expect, which is why it
shares the type's line.

Where the eyebrow would only repeat the name, the line carries that type's one
identifying fact instead. A dropped pin is the whole of that case. Its name is a
label, so a verbatim eyebrow would print *Dropped pin* above *Dropped pin*, and its
coordinates are the only thing that says which pin it is. In the code that is the
`locatedByPoint` branch in `domain/poi/types/place.tsx`.

A street address is a location fact, not an identity one, so it does not go in the
header. A campground's address survives at Full, in `contact`.

## Recorded gaps

Two things are missing from phase 1. They are stated here as gaps and are not
worked around.

- **No photographs anywhere.** The sheet omits `hero`, and nothing else renders a
  photograph, so the Full list above promises photographs the sheet does not yet
  show. The fix is not to reorder the blocks.
- **No street address for `place`, `charger` or `group`.** The body block that
  should hold it does not exist yet, and neither does the charger's common-site
  label or a park group's state and manager. The answer is that block, not
  overloading the header's single line.

Both are open work. Neither is a decision that the information does not matter.
